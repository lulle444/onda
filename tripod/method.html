Method

One number, shown in full.

How a basket's level is calculated, where every price comes from, and the ways it can mislead you.

01 · Price

Each leg's price

For every stock token we take the pool with the most liquidity on Robinhood Chain (via DexScreener) and use its USD price and its 24-hour change.

02 · Level

The basket's level

Level = 100 × Σ weight × (1 + 24h change). So 100 means flat against 24 hours ago, 103 means the basket is up 3%.

03 · Missing legs

When a leg isn't live

If a token isn't on the chain or has no priced pool, it's left out and the other legs are scaled up so the weights still add to 100%. The basket page says which.

Risk and limits

Thin pools move on small trades

Some stock tokens have only a few thousand dollars of liquidity. One trade can swing their pool price, and with it the basket. The liquidity column tells you how much to trust a move.

Outside US hours, the reference freezes

Robinhood trades stock tokens Sunday 8 pm to Friday 8 pm New York time. Outside that, pool prices can drift from the frozen share price, which shows up as a gap on the Tokens page.

It's a 24-hour view, not a history

Levels are rebased every moment against 24 hours earlier. They don't track a basket from the day it was made.

Nothing here is tradeable

Tripod is read-only. There are no contracts, no positions and no leverage. To act on a basket you'd buy the legs yourself, on Robinhood or on-chain.

Questions

Where does the data come from?

Robinhood's public stock-token API (the token list, logos and bid/ask), DexScreener (pool prices, 24h change, liquidity and volume on Robinhood Chain), the public Robinhood Chain RPC (block height) and CoinGecko (ETH price). Our server fetches them together and caches the result for 60 seconds.

How often does it update?

Every minute while the page is open.

Where is my custom basket stored?

Only in its link. The legs, weights and name are in the URL, so nothing is saved on a server and anyone with the link sees the same basket.

Is this investment advice?

No. It's a way to look at prices. Do your own research.
